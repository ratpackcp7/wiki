# AGI is here (and I feel fine)
**Author:** Robin Sloan
**Date:** January 4, 2026

## Summary
Robin Sloan argues that AGI has already arrived. The "General" in AGI is the operative word — unlike previous narrow AI (AlphaGo, AlphaFold), modern LLMs possess open-ended capability that surprised even their creators.

### Key points:
- The 2020 GPT-3 paper (Language Models are Few-Shot Learners) identified as the true AGI moment
- "If you find yourself parsing different flavors of generality, it's AGI." (Robin's Razor)
- Industry insiders resist the AGI label strategically — maintaining the "ever-receding goal" allows demanding more money and electricity
- Sloan proposes users should unilaterally declare the goal reached to force the dialogue to "what now?"
- Models have "prodigious immediate generality" vs. humans' "implacable eventual generality"
- François Chollet's 2019 paper argues universality isn't required for generality — current models meet the practical bar
- Historical parallel to 1970s/80s personal computer boom: realized visions, but no utopia
- The arrival of the technology is the beginning of the work, not the end
