# Giving University Exams in the Age Of Chatbots

**Source:** [Simon Willison's Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/) | **Date:** 20th January 2026

[Original post](https://ploum.net/2026-01-19-exam-with-chatbots.html) ([via](https://lobste.rs/s/parmy3/giving_university_exams_age_chatbots))

Detailed and thoughtful description of an open-book and open-chatbot exam run by [Ploum](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Dricot) at École Polytechnique de Louvain for an "Open Source Strategies" class.

Students were told they could use chatbots during the exam but they had to announce their intention to do so in advance, share their prompts and take full accountability for any mistakes they made.

Only 3 out of 60 students chose to use chatbots. Ploum surveyed half of the class to help understand their motivations.

## Primary Source: Giving University Exams in the Age of Chatbots

**Source:** [ploum.net](https://ploum.net/2026-01-19-exam-with-chatbots.html) | **Author:** Ploum | **Date:** 2026-01-19

### Overview
Ploum, a professor at École Polytechnique de Louvain, details a pedagogical experiment in his "Open Source Strategies" course. He explores how allowing (but regulating) AI usage during exams reveals significant correlations between student performance, critical thinking, and their relationship with technology.

### The "Subversive" Exam Rules
To reduce stress and maximize learning, the exam follows unconventional guidelines:
1. **Open Resources:** Full internet access and laptops allowed.
2. **No Formal Time Limit:** Though excessive time is noted as a symptom of lack of preparation.
3. **Collaboration:** Students may discuss topics with each other if they face similar problems.
4. **Student-Led Questions:** Students can bring their own prepared exam questions.
5. **Dress Code:** "Come dressed for the exam you dream of taking!" (Results included a T-Rex suit, pajamas, and Minnie Mouse).

### The Chatbot Experiment
Students were given a choice regarding LLM usage, framed by strict accountability:

**Option A:** No chatbots. Traditional web search only.
**Option B:** Chatbots allowed with three conditions:
1. Disclose every time information comes from a bot.
2. Share the prompts used.
3. **Identify and explain mistakes** made by the chatbot.

> "Mistakes made by chatbots will be considered more important than honest human mistakes, resulting in the loss of more points. If you use chatbots, you should be held accountable for the output."

### Student Choices and Performance Clusters
Out of 60 students, **57 chose Option A (No Chatbots)**.

| Group | Reasoning | Grade Range |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Preference** | Prefer pride in own work; find verifying LLMs takes too much time. | **15–19** (Top students) |
| **Pragmatic** | Decided LLMs weren't necessary for this specific subject. | **12–16** |
| **Never Use** | Dislike the interaction; find bots "stupid" or annoying. | **~13** (Middle ground) |
| **Heavy Users** | Use them daily but **feared the accountability constraints** of the exam. | **8–11** (Worst performers) |

> "Can chatbots help? Yes, if you know how to use them. But if you do, chances are you don't need chatbots."

### The "Stream of Consciousness" Requirement
A new 2026 requirement: students kept a live text file of their thoughts during the exam. No deleting, no correcting, no back-tracking, no copy-pasting. Ploum was able to "save" several failing students by seeing that they understood the material in their notes but struggled with the oral/formal articulation due to stress.

### Generational Trends and Institutional Critique
- Students are increasingly terrified of being accused of cheating
- Students often conflate Git (the tool) with GitHub (the platform)
- University mail migration to Outlook destroyed the email experience (15 submissions ended up in spam)

### Final Philosophy
> "Progress is each generation going further than the previous one while learning from the mistakes of your elders. I'm there to tell you about my own mistakes and the mistakes of my generation."
