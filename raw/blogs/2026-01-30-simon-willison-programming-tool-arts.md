# We gotta talk about AI as a programming tool for the arts

# Summary: AI as a Programming Tool for the Arts

**Source:** [Simon Willison's Weblog](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jan/30/a-programming-tool-for-the-arts/)
**Key Figure:** Chris Ashworth (CEO of QLab, founder of The Voxel theater)
**Date:** January 30, 2026

## Overview
Simon Willison highlights a shift in perspective from Chris Ashworth, a veteran software developer for the arts. Despite long-standing skepticism toward generative AI, Ashworth recently experienced a "career-changing moment" using **Claude Code** to develop niche software for the theater community.

## The "Claude Opus" Moment
Ashworth used AI to build a custom lighting design application for a project with an extremely small audience (only three people).
*   **The Efficiency Gain:** A project that would normally be impossible to justify—given the 20 years of professional energy required to reach that level of polish—was completed in just a **few days**.
*   **The Use Case:** Creating highly specialized, "unprofitable" tools for artists that would otherwise never exist due to time and resource constraints.

## Key Insights on AI-Assisted Programming
Ashworth offers a nuanced view of AI as a "power tool" for developers, emphasizing that it amplifies existing skills rather than replacing the need for expertise.

### 1. The Skill Multiplier
> "It can't make you a fundamentally better programmer than you already are. It can take a person who is a bad programmer and make them faster at making bad programs. And I think it can take a person who is a good programmer and... make them faster at making good programs."

### 2. The Danger of "Blind Shipping"
Ashworth expresses concern over developers who deploy code they do not comprehend:
> "You see programmers out there saying, 'I'm shipping code I haven't looked at and don't understand.' I'm terrified by that. I think that's awful."

### 3. The "Power Tool" Analogy
He compares AI to a workshop full of dangerous but efficient machinery:
> "I wouldn't want to send an untrained person into a room full of power tools because they might chop off their fingers. But if someone who knows how to use tools has the option to have both hand tools and a power saw and a power drill and a lathe, there's a lot of work they can do with those tools at a lot faster speed."

## Conclusion
While Ashworth maintains a level of "rational distrust" and acknowledges the ethical tensions surrounding the technology, he concludes that AI-assisted programming is a transformative shift for the arts. It allows for the creation of high-quality, bespoke tools for niche artistic applications that were previously economically or temporally unfeasible.

**Related Entities:**
*   **QLab:** Industry-standard macOS software for multimedia playback in live theater.
*   **The Voxel:** A Baltimore-based theater, research lab, and teaching hub used by QLab.
*   **Claude Code:** The specific AI toolset used by Ashworth for this project.
