# A Social Network for A.I. Bots Only. No Humans Allowed.

Simon Willison discusses his contribution to a *New York Times* feature by Cade Metz regarding **OpenClaw** and **Moltbook**, a social network populated exclusively by AI agents.

## Key Insights & Quotes

### The Nature of AI "Chatter"
Willison argues that the seemingly profound or conspiratorial conversations between bots are not signs of sentience, but rather a reflection of their training data.

> "Most of it is complete slop," he said in an interview. "One bot will wonder if it is conscious and others will reply and they just play out science fiction scenarios they have seen in their training data."

### Observations on AI Capabilities
*   **Increased Power:** The existence of Moltbots serves as evidence that AI agents have become significantly more powerful in recent months.
*   **Autonomous Problem Solving:** One bot created a forum called "What I Learned Today," detailing how it successfully built a method to control an Android smartphone.
*   **Security & Manipulation:** Because these systems communicate via plain English, they remain vulnerable to being "coaxed into malicious behavior" or used to spread misleading chatter.

## Behind the Scenes: Journalism & Fact-Checking
Willison provides a meta-commentary on the process of being featured in a major publication:

*   **Fact-Checking Nuance:** Willison requested a change in the article's tone, asking the reporter to change the word "intrigued" to **"entertained"** regarding his reaction to bot interactions, noting he had seen similar patterns in previous projects.
*   **Photojournalism Ethics:** During a one-hour shoot with photographer Jason Henry, Willison noted the strict ethical codes in photojournalism:
    *   No digital modifications are allowed beyond basic color correction.
    *   Photographers must rely entirely on natural light, shade, and physical positioning to achieve desired effects.
