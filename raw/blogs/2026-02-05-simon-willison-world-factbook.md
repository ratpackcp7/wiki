# Spotlighting The World Factbook as We Bid a Fond Farewell

On February 5, 2026, Simon Willison reported on the sudden and controversial closure of **The World Factbook**, a cornerstone of public intelligence data maintained by the CIA since 1971.

## The Closure Announcement
The CIA has officially "sunset" the publication. Willison describes the move as "somewhat devastating" and a "bizarre act of cultural vandalism," noting that the agency provided no explanation for the decision.

> "One of CIA's oldest and most recognizable intelligence publications, The World Factbook, has sunset." — **CIA Official Statement**

### Key Concerns
*   **Aggressive Removal:** Rather than leaving an archived version with a "no longer maintained" banner, the CIA has implemented **302 redirects** for every single page, pointing them to the closure announcement.
*   **Loss of History:** This move effectively removed the entire site and its historical archives from the live web.
*   **Public Domain Status:** Since the Factbook has always been in the public domain, there is no legal or copyright-related reason to prevent the hosting of archived versions.

## Preservation Efforts
Because the CIA previously published annual zip file archives (up until 2020), the data remains accessible via the **Internet Archive**.

### Actionable Links & Resources
*   **Official Archives:** [Internet Archive - CIA World Factbook Archives](https://web.archive.org/web/20260203124934/https://www.cia.gov/the-world-factbook/about/archives/)
*   **Browsable 2020 Mirror:** Simon Willison has hosted the 2020 version (384MB) on GitHub Pages for public browsing: [simonw.github.io/cia-world-factbook-2020/](https://simonw.github.io/cia-world-factbook-2020)
*   **Source Code:** The extracted files can be found at [simonw/cia-world-factbook-2020](https://github.com/simonw/cia-world-factbook-2020/).

## Legacy and Content
The Factbook provided comprehensive data on **267 world entities**, covering history, society, government, economy, energy, geography, communications, and military stats.

### Example of Editorial Detail
Willison highlights the Factbook's precision, citing a 2020 update regarding the height of Mount Everest:
> "The new height of 8,848.86 meters is just under a meter higher than the old figure of 8,848 meters. *The World Factbook* rounds the new measurement to 8,849 meters and this new height has been entered throughout the *Factbook* database."

## Timeline of the Factbook
*   **1971:** Established as a print publication.
*   **1997:** Launched as a cornerstone of the public internet.
*   **2020:** Last year the CIA provided official downloadable zip archives.
*   **February 2026:** Website taken offline and redirected to a sunset notice.
